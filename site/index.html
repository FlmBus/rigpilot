The arrangement

Your whole show, on one timeline.

Reference audio on top, one MIDI track per device below. Place events by ear on a BPM grid — preset changes, stomps, volume fades — exactly where the song needs them.

Three event types

Everything your rig does fits in three shapes.

One-ShotFire and forget

Preset changes, scene switches, tap tempo. Fires once, exactly on its tick — rendered as a diamond marker.

HoldOn for the block

Stomps, sustains, anything with an on and an off. Engages at block start, disengages at block end. Drag the edges to resize.

AutomationCurves over time

Volume fades, wah sweeps, whammy dives. A breakpoint curve targeting a MIDI CC, rendered to a smooth CC stream on export.

How it works

From manual to .mid in four steps.

No more digging through MIDI implementation charts. Device definitions describe what your gear can do in plain language — you just say when.

  1. Import your song

    Drop in the mix or stems as reference audio, set the BPM, done.

  2. Add a track per device

    Pick a device definition, set the MIDI channel. One track, one device, one exported file.

  3. Drag commands onto the timeline

    "Stomp A", "Select Rig", "Master Volume" — from the palette, by name. Snap grid, multi-select, copy/paste, undo.

  4. Export & play

    Standard MIDI files with latency compensation baked in — slow preset loads land on the beat, not after it. Works in Studio One, Reaper, anything.

Device library

Speaks your gear's language already.

Definitions are simple XML files — like fixture definitions in lighting software. Missing yours? It's an afternoon of work with the MIDI chart, and contributions are welcome.
